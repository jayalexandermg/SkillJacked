# Humanize Brand Content With Named Personalities

Give commodity/brand accounts a recognizable human face by putting named employees front-and-center in content instead of an anonymous corporate voice.

## Quick Start
When a brand account's posts feel generic or "AI slop," identify one or more real employees willing to be the recurring named voice/face of the content, and reframe posts as coming from that person ("Tiffany from [Brand]") rather than the brand itself.

## Core Workflow

1. **Trigger**: Brand/company account content underperforms personal creator content or gets flagged internally as "corporate," "flat," or "AI-generated sounding."
2. **Identify candidate personalities**: Look beyond the CEO/founder — scan the team for employees who are comfortable being visible and have an authentic voice (customer service, store staff, product team, etc.).
3. **Assign ownership**: Give a specific named employee ongoing credit/visibility as the face behind a content stream (e.g., posts, replies, videos) instead of rotating anonymous "brand voice" copy.
4. **Reframe commodity content through that person**: Route routine/commodity brand messages (promotions, product info, customer interactions) through the named persona so the brand's ordinary content gains an individual attributable identity.
5. **Repeat and reinforce**: Keep the same named person(s) consistently associated with the content so audiences build familiarity/trust with an individual, not an institution.

## Techniques

- **Named-employee-as-persona pattern**: Instead of "Taco Bell announces...", frame it as "Tiffany from Taco Bell shares...". The named individual becomes the recognizable throughline across posts.
- **Non-executive personality sourcing**: IF the CEO/founder is not naturally camera-friendly or doesn't scale to cover all content needs, THEN identify other employees (not just leadership) who can become the named personality for specific content lines.
- **Commodity-to-personal conversion**: IF brand content is inherently commodity/generic (standard promos, standard announcements), THEN attribute it to a specific named person to make it feel individually authored rather than institutionally generated.

## Anti-Patterns

- NEVER rely solely on the CEO/founder as the only possible "personality" for brand content — this bottlenecks humanization and doesn't scale.
- NEVER let brand content stay attributed to the faceless company name when a named-persona reframe is available, because anonymous corporate voice reads as "AI slop" even when human-written.
- AVOID rotating unnamed staff anonymously behind the same content stream — the consistency of a *named* individual is what builds recognizability and trust.

## Edge Cases & Error Handling

- IF no employee is willing/available to be a public-facing named personality, THEN this technique cannot be applied as-is; consider identifying at least one comfortable spokesperson before scaling branded content.
- IF the brand has multiple content lines (e.g., support, marketing, product), THEN consider assigning different named personalities per line rather than forcing one person to cover everything.
