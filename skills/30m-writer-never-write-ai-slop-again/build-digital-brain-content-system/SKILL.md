# Build a Digital Brain / Personal Language Model

Turn your best approved language and points of view into a reusable knowledge base so AI stops inventing generic content and starts repeating your voice.

## Quick Start
When a user asks AI to write content from scratch every time, tell them: capture your best phrasing once it's approved, store it as a reference the AI can pull from, and have future generations quote/adapt that stored language instead of generating new copy blind.

## Core Workflow

1. **Identify the core moat**: IF a user believes "AI removes moats" (e.g., "SaaS has no moat anymore"), THEN reframe: the moat is having a distinct, defensible point of view — not the product mechanics.

2. **Crystallize points of view**: When the user has a strong opinion or belief about their space, capture it as a standalone, quotable statement (a "point of view" asset) — not just a topic idea. Example pattern demonstrated: reframe a common claim ("SaaS has no moat in the AI era") into a sharper counter-claim ("SaaS has a moat when you have a point of view").

3. **Approve language before reuse**: When content (a sentence, phrase, or framing) is written and gets approved/resonates, THEN log it into the digital brain as reusable language — don't let it disappear after one use.

4. **Let AI repeat, not invent**: When generating new content, instruct the AI to pull from the stored bank of approved points of view and phrasing rather than generating fresh, generic language each time.

5. **Compound over time**: Treat each approved piece of language as a permanent addition to the system. IF the system only stores ideas/topics but not exact approved wording, THEN it will not compound — the wording itself is the asset.

## Techniques

- **Point-of-view crystallization pattern**: Take a common industry claim, then produce a sharper, contrarian, or clarifying restatement of it. This restated claim becomes a stored asset.
  - Input: generic claim ("X no longer has a moat in the AI era")
  - Output: sharpened point of view ("X has a moat when you have Y")
- **Reuse over regeneration**: When drafting new content, first check the digital brain for an existing approved line that fits, and adapt/quote it rather than writing a brand-new sentence.

## Anti-Patterns

- NEVER let AI regenerate a point of view from scratch every time — this produces generic "AI slop" instead of a consistent, recognizable voice.
- NEVER treat content generation as a one-off task — approved language that isn't captured is lost and must be reinvented later, wasting the compounding effect.
- AVOID storing only vague "ideas" or "topics" in the digital brain — store the actual approved phrasing/wording, since that is what carries the voice.

## Edge Cases & Error Handling

- IF the user has no existing point of view on their space, THEN help them form one first (via the crystallization pattern) before attempting to build the digital brain — there's nothing to store yet.
- IF a user's content still sounds generic despite having AI tools, THEN the likely cause is the absence of a stored, approved point-of-view/language bank — the fix is capturing and reusing approved language, not better prompting alone.
