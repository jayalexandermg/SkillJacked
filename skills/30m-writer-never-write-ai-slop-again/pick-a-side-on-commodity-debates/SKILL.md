# Pick a Side on Commodity Debates

Force a clear, personal stance on the standard opposing arguments in a niche before writing, so content isn't generic commodity slop.

## Quick Start
Before drafting any piece on a popular topic, ask: "What is the well-known debate here, and which side do I actually believe?" Write the stance down as a single sentence (e.g., "Skip the daily coffee and invest it" vs. "Buy the coffee, cutting small joys doesn't move the needle") and use that stance as the filter for every example, anecdote, and argument in the draft.

## Core Workflow
1. **Identify the niche's commodity debate.** When a topic is popular enough that "everyone talks about it," there is almost always a standard pro/con framing (commodity content). Name both sides explicitly.
2. **Interrogate your actual belief.** Ask: do I genuinely agree with the common/default side, or do I disagree? Do not accept the default side just because it's common.
3. **Commit to an explicit stance.** Write one clear sentence stating your position on the debate. If you can't state it in one sentence, you haven't picked a side yet — return to step 2.
4. **Use the stance as a filter.** When AI or your own draft generates examples, anecdotes, or supporting arguments, check each one against your stance. If an example only makes sense under the opposing/default view, cut or rewrite it.
5. **Inject personality/original content on top of the stance.** The commodity debate is the entry point; the personal stance plus your own experience/opinion is what converts commodity content into differentiated content.

## Techniques
- **Debate-naming template:** "In [niche], the standard debate is [Side A] vs. [Side B]. I believe [Side A/B] because [reason]." Use this sentence as a prompt/system-instruction anchor when generating AI drafts.
- **Content-type layering:** Treat writing as stacked layers — commodity content (the known debate/topic) → personality content (your voice, stance, experience) → original content (new synthesis/insight). Each layer adds leverage; skipping the personality layer is what produces slop.
- **Stance-check pass:** After a draft is generated, re-read every example/anecdote and ask "does this only work if I believed the OTHER side?" If yes, replace it.

## Anti-Patterns
- NEVER let a draft present "both sides equally" on a commodity debate without your own stance — that reads as generic AI slop because it commits to nothing.
- NEVER accept AI-generated default examples uncritically; AI defaults to the most common (commodity) framing, which may not match your actual belief.
- NEVER write about a popular topic assuming the reader already knows your position — state it explicitly, since the stance itself is what differentiates the piece.

## Edge Cases & Error Handling
- If you genuinely have no strong opinion on the niche's debate, that is a signal you may not be the right person to write on this topic yet — form a real opinion first, don't fake a stance.
- If the AI/draft keeps reverting to generic examples after you've stated your stance, explicitly instruct it to filter all examples through the stated stance sentence, and manually strip any leftover "both sides" hedging language.
