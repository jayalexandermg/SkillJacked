# Engineer Voice Through Word Choice and Sentence Structure

Voice mismatch is not mystical — diagnose it as concrete variables (perspective, word choice, sentence structure) and fix with targeted swaps.

## Quick Start
When a user says "this doesn't sound like me," FIRST check pronoun perspective: is the content written from "you should..." (commodity advice) or "I think.../I did..." (personality)? Rewrite "you"-framed sentences into first-person "I" framing before touching anything else.

## Core Workflow
1. **When** a user flags content as not sounding like them, **then** scan for perspective: count instances of "you should," "you need to," "you have to" vs. "I think," "I did," "I believe."
2. **When** the content is dominated by "you"-framed imperatives, **then** flag it as commodity content — it reads like generic advice everyone already repeats, not like a specific person's viewpoint.
3. **When** rewriting, **then** convert "you should do X" statements into "I did X" / "I think X" statements, anchoring claims in the writer's own experience or opinion rather than generic instruction.
4. **When** the perspective is already first-person but still feels off, **then** move to the next tunable variable — word choice — and look for a single word or phrase that reads as inauthentic or overly generic; test replacing just that word before rewriting whole sentences.
5. **When** word choice is fixed and voice still feels wrong, **then** examine sentence length/structure as the third variable (this excerpt establishes perspective and word choice as the primary levers; treat sentence structure as the next diagnostic layer).

## Techniques
- **Perspective Test**: For any sentence, ask "Is this framed as universal instruction (you) or personal experience/opinion (I)?" Universal instruction = commodity; personal framing = personality.
  - Commodity: "You should do this."
  - Personality: "I think this would be a great startup idea."
- **Single-Word Swap Principle**: Do not assume a full rewrite is needed when voice feels off. Treat voice mismatch as debuggable at the word level — isolate and swap the specific word/phrase causing the mismatch before rewriting entire passages.
- **Ownership Framing**: When converting advice-style content, attribute the claim to the writer specifically (e.g., "I think X" instead of "X is true" or "you should do X") so the reader experiences it as "what [person] thinks," not generic consensus.

## Anti-Patterns
- NEVER treat "doesn't sound like me" as an unsolvable, purely intuitive problem — it decomposes into perspective, word choice, and sentence structure.
- NEVER leave content in "you should" framing when the goal is personality-driven writing — this defaults to commodity advice that sounds like "everyone."
- NEVER default to rewriting an entire paragraph/post when the issue may be isolated to a single word or phrase; check for a targeted swap first.

## Edge Cases & Error Handling
- If the user's content is legitimately meant to be instructional/universal (e.g., a how-to guide), "you"-framing may be intentional — apply the personality test only when the goal is personal voice/brand content, not generic instruction.
- If switching from "you" to "I" makes claims feel unsupported or arrogant, pair the "I" framing with a brief personal anchor (what the writer did/experienced) rather than a bare opinion statement.
